Forgeline helps you find your way into projects, not just look at them: topic pages across forges, first issues to contribute to, collections of repositories and a year of activity on profiles. Pull requests and issues can now be reviewed and triaged in full, and wikis and discussions live in the app. It needs Android 12 or later.

## Highlights

- **Topics.** A repository's topics open a page of what else is tagged so, the most starred of GitHub, Codeberg and gitlab.com mixed, or one forge at a time. It works without signing in, and topic links from those three forges open there too.
- **First issues.** From Trending's header: open issues their maintainers marked for a first contribution, in the repositories you starred lately and in today's Trending, on every forge. Projects name that label in many ways (`good first issue`, `easy-fix`, `Kind/Good First Issue`…); Forgeline reads each repository's own.
- **Collections.** Lists of repositories from any forge, kept on your phone. Put a repository in one from the bookmark at the top of its page, and find them from the You tab. Your GitHub lists can be imported; importing again adds what they gained and leaves your changes alone.
- **A year of activity on profiles.** A square a day, the busier the more marigold, as GitHub (signed in), GitLab and Forgejo count it.
- **Your repositories first in Search**, found on the phone as you type, before the forges are even asked.
- **Pull requests, decided at their end.** After its checks, a pull request gathers what is left to do: review, ask for reviewers, mark it draft or ready, bring its branch up to date, merge the ways the repository allows (now, or once checks pass) and delete the branch. What stands in a merge's way is named.
- **Reviewing in full.** Review threads to answer, resolve or dismiss; remarks on one line, several or a whole file; suggestions shown as the change they would make and committed to the branch (GitHub and GitLab); reactions on remarks, and your own rewritten or taken back; files marked as viewed.
- **Triage in full.** New issues from the repository's templates and, on GitHub, its issue forms, with their labels; a pull request's description from its template; a new label from the picker; hiding a comment with a reason; an issue's type, parent and sub-issues; the pull requests that would close an issue, and the commit that did; saved replies in comments; subscribing from a conversation's end; a pull request's target branch.
- **Wikis and discussions in the app.** A repository's wiki is read in the app on all three forges, and links to its pages open there. On GitHub you can now comment on a discussion, reply under a comment and pick the answer.
- **Runs.** GitHub runs show the report they kept in their artifacts, and a new setting opens a failed job straight at the step it failed at.

## Fixes

- The Feed loaded twice on each refresh.
- gitlab.com's job logs, now stamped line by line, lost their step names and error counts.
- A repository with nothing pushed showed an error; it now says so.
- The Inbox's swipes and filters are explained the first time they're met.

## Known limitations

- Pictures in private repositories don't load in the code browser.
- A GitHub profile's activity needs you signed in to GitHub: its API only gives it to an account.
- On Codeberg, a remark on several lines or a whole file, viewed files, hidden comments, sub-issues and issue types aren't offered (Forgejo's API has none of them), a suggestion can't be committed, and a remark you take back stays.
- An organisation's new repository in the Feed is signed by the organisation, not by the member who made it, and can take up to 30 minutes to appear.
- Marking a GitLab to-do as read doesn't last (GitLab only knows pending and done).
- Codeberg gives no ETag, so its answers are downloaded again each time.

No backend, no analytics, no trackers: the app talks directly to your forges. Forgeline is free software under the GPL-3.0.
